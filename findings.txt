HR EMPLOYEE ATTRITION ANALYSIS - FINDINGS
==========================================
Dataset: IBM HR Analytics Employee Attrition (1,470 employees, 35 columns)
Attrition rate = employees who left / employees in the group
All numbers below matched across Python, Excel, SQL and Power BI.


OVERALL
-------
- 1,470 employees, 237 left, 1,233 stayed.
- Overall attrition rate: 16.1% (about 1 in 6 employees).
- Data quality: 0 missing values, 0 duplicate rows.
- 3 columns (EmployeeCount, StandardHours, Over18) had the same value for
  every employee, so they were dropped along with EmployeeNumber (an ID).


Q1: WHICH DEPARTMENT HAS THE HIGHEST ATTRITION?
-----------------------------------------------
- Sales: 20.6%
- Human Resources: 19.0%   (19.05% in Excel before rounding)
- Research & Development: 13.8%
- Company average: 16.1%
=> Sales and HR are above the company average, R&D is below.


Q2: DOES OVERTIME AFFECT ATTRITION?
-----------------------------------
- Employees working overtime: about 30.5%  (127 of 416 left)
- Employees not working overtime: about 10.4%
=> Overtime workers leave at roughly 3 times the rate of those who do not.
   This is the strongest single signal in the analysis.


Q3: IS ATTRITION LINKED TO INCOME?
----------------------------------
- Low income (up to 3,000):         28.6%   (113 of 395 left)
- Medium income (3,001 - 6,000):    12.7%   (66 of 519 left)
- High income (6,001 - 10,000):     12.0%   (33 of 275 left)
- Very High income (above 10,000):   8.9%   (25 of 281 left)
=> Low-income employees leave at about 3 times the rate of the highest earners.


Q4: WHICH AGE GROUPS AND TENURE BANDS LEAVE MOST?
-------------------------------------------------
Age group
- 18-25:  35.8%   (44 of 123 left)
- 26-35:  19.1%   (116 of 606 left)
- 36-45:   9.2%   (43 of 468 left)
- 46-55:  11.5%   (26 of 226 left)
- 56+:    17.0%   (8 of 47 left)   <- small group, treat with caution

Tenure (years at the company)
- 0-2 years:    29.8%   (102 of 342 left)
- 3-5 years:    13.8%   (60 of 434 left)
- 6-10 years:   12.3%   (55 of 448 left)
- 11+ years:     8.1%   (20 of 246 left)
=> Young and new employees leave the most. Attrition falls as tenure grows.


Q5: LEAVERS VS STAYERS (AVERAGE VALUES)
---------------------------------------
                         Stayed (No)   Left (Yes)
- Job satisfaction (1-4):     2.78         2.47
- Work-life balance (1-4):    2.78         2.66
- Years since promotion:      2.23         1.95
- Monthly income:          6,832.74     4,787.09
- Distance from home:         8.92        10.63
=> Leavers earn about 30% less, report lower job satisfaction, and live
   slightly further from work.
=> Years since last promotion did NOT explain attrition: leavers actually had
   slightly FEWER years since promotion. The hypothesis "long wait for a
   promotion drives people out" was not supported by this data.


MAIN CONCLUSION
---------------
Attrition is highest among young, new, low-paid employees, among employees who
work overtime, and in the Sales and HR departments.


RECOMMENDATIONS
---------------
1. Strengthen onboarding and mentoring in the first two years.
2. Review pay for the low-income band.
3. Review overtime workload, especially for new and young employees.
4. Investigate why Sales and HR lose a higher share of employees
   (for example, exit interviews).


LIMITATIONS
-----------
- Correlation is not causation: the analysis shows links, not causes.
- Age, tenure and income overlap (young employees are often newer and paid
  less), so they may be one story seen three ways.
- Small groups (for example 56+ with only 47 employees) give unstable rates.
- The data is a single snapshot, so trends over time cannot be analysed.
- It is a practice dataset, so findings are for learning, not real company advice.
